MISSION & STRUCTURE

Engineered to Solve Campus Realities

"29th Solution – Edition 1" bridges theoretical electronics engineering and real-world campus implementation.

Hosted by the ECE Association of PSG Institute of Technology and Applied Research, this initiative challenges students to build deployable systems using embedded controllers, sensor networks, signal analysis, IoT, and intelligent software to eliminate day-to-day operational pain points.

Teams can either select one of the 7 pre-identified problem statements curated directly by the ECE Association or pioneer a novel hardware/software concept under Track 8: Open Innovation.

THE CORE MANTRA
"Think. Innovate. Solve."

Department Exclusivity

All team members must be ECE students at PSG iTech. Teams can comprise students across 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and 4th years to encourage peer learning.

Zero Initial Prototyping Barrier

No hardware assembly or component purchase is required for initial shortlisting. Submit your circuit simulation, architecture, or code proof to advance.

Rules & Participation Guidelines

Everything you need to know about team structure, eligibility, original submission requirements, and prototype expectations.

Each team must consist of a minimum of 3 members and a maximum of 5 members. Solo entries and teams exceeding 5 members are strictly not permitted to ensure balanced collaboration. Each individual may participate in only one team.
All team members must be students of the ECE Department at PSG Institute of Technology and Applied Research. Members can be from any year of study (1st, 2nd, 3rd, or Final year). Inter-year teams combining seniors and juniors are encouraged.
Teams must choose one problem statement from the given list (ECE-PS01 to ECE-PS07). Alternatively, under Open Innovation (ECE-PS08), teams may independently identify and frame a real, practical challenge faced by the campus community.
Submit your idea summary along with a Proof of Concept (PoC) [IF Possible] on or before 23/09/2026. The PoC demonstrates the viability of your design and can include circuit simulations, block diagrams, pseudocode, UI wireframes, or preliminary test data.
Each team may submit only one idea. Multiple idea submissions or participation of an individual in multiple teams are not permitted.
A working prototype is NOT required for the initial submission. Do not rush to assemble physical hardware before shortlisting. Only teams shortlisted after the Round 1 evaluation will be called upon to develop a functional working prototype for the Final Display.
The proposed engineering solution and implementation must be original work. You are completely permitted and encouraged to leverage existing technologies, microcontroller boards, sensor modules, communication APIs, and open-source libraries to build upon.

Evaluation Criteria

Every submission and subsequent prototype demonstration is objectively measured across six key technical pillars.

CRIT_01 // FOUNDATION

Problem Understanding

Clarity in defining the root cause of the problem, operational pain-points, stakeholder context, and campus relevance.

CRIT_02 // CREATIVITY

Innovation

Novelty of the solution approach, creative hardware/software architecture, and differentiation from trivial or existing methods.

CRIT_03 // VIABILITY

Feasibility

Realistic implementation within constraints, component availability, budget practicality, power consumption, and deployability.

CRIT_04 // OUTCOME

Practical Impact

Measurable positive impact on campus operations, energy savings, reliability enhancement, or user convenience for students and faculty.

CRIT_05 // SCALABLE

Scalability and Adaptability

Potential to expand, adapt, or integrate the solution for larger deployments, additional use cases, or future improvements.

CRIT_06 // Presentation For Finale

Presentation

Articulation during question-and-answer session, technical confidence, schematic clarity, and live prototype demonstration at Final Display.
